## Premise
A dial on your wrist scales how hard Earth pulls on you (your weight). Your muscles stay the same.

## Facts (own calcs)
- Float: a ~70 kg body (density ~1010 kg/m³) displaces ~0.068 m³; × air 1.2 kg/m³ ≈ 80 g of air. Weigh less than that and
  buoyancy wins: you rise like a balloon. A deck of cards ≈ 94 g ("less than a deck of cards" ≈ 80 g, rounded).
- Jump: the same leg push gives height ∝ 1/weight: a 0.5 m standing jump → ~1 m at half, ~5 m at a tenth (a one-storey roof).
- Grip: friction = μ × weight, so at a tenth your shoes skid like on ice.
- Wind: drag ½ρv²CdA = 0.5 × 1.2 × 10² × ~0.8 m² ≈ 48 N at 10 m/s (36 km/h) ≈ the weight of 4-5 kg.
- Heavy: ×10 ≈ 700 kg, nearly a small car (~900-1000 kg); a passenger lift is typically rated ~630 kg (8 people).
- Equator: g pole 9.832 vs equator 9.780 m/s² → 0.53 % less (spin + equatorial bulge: farther from the centre).
