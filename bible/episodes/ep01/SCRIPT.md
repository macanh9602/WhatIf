## Premise
No light between 495 and 570 nm exists anywhere: the Sun simply stops making it. Everything else in physics stays the same.

## Facts (checked; own calcs in 10 nm steps, CIE V(λ)/V'(λ) × AM1.5)
- White sunlight minus green = magenta/pink. The sky stays blue (scattering unchanged).
- Leaves use red + blue best; green is the colour they use least (McCree), so they reflect it most (≈10-20 % vs ≈5 %).
  No green → leaves reflect almost nothing visible → near black. Photosynthesis still runs on red + blue.
- Screen white = R+G+B sub-pixels → white shows magenta. Traffic "go" LEDs ≈ 505 nm → dark; red/amber still work.
- Green band: 25 % of visible sunlight energy, 61 % of photopic brightness, 55 % of scotopic (night) sensitivity.
- Yellow things reflect red + green → banana/gold lose the green half → orange / copper.
- Visible ≈ 43 % of solar energy → green ≈ 11 % of ALL sunlight. Equilibrium T ∝ S^¼ → ≈ 8 K colder before feedbacks;
  last glacial maximum ≈ 6 K colder. Ice-albedo feedback: energy-balance models (Budyko/Sellers) put the snowball
  threshold at a few-to-10 % drop, so "could" freeze over (hedged).

## Script
1. If green vanished from the universe, every forest on Earth would instantly turn black.
2. Imagine opening your curtains on a sunny morning, and the whole street is glowing pink.
3. Sunlight is every color mixed together, and without green, the red and blue left over make pink.
4. Your lawn looks like burnt toast, and your neighbor is mowing a black carpet.
5. You grab your phone for answers, but the screen is purple, because every white pixel was secretly part green.
6. At the crossing, the light shows red, then yellow, then nothing, and nobody knows if nothing means go.
7. And the whole day looks dim, like the sun has been turned down.
8. That's not your imagination.
9. Green was only a quarter of sunlight, but your eyes are tuned to the middle of the rainbow, right where green used to be.
10. So green made more than half of all the brightness you see, and now it's like wearing sunglasses you can never take off.
11. Your banana looks orange, your gold ring looks like copper, and every green frog and parrot on Earth turns black.
12. Which, to be fair, is perfect camouflage on a black leaf.
13. Nights get worse too, because the cells you see with in the dark were most sensitive to blue-green.
14. Moonlight loses half its power on your eyes, and a walk home feels like a walk through ink.
15. But all of that is just how the world looks. Here's the part that actually hurts.
16. Green wasn't only a color. It was energy, about a tenth of everything the sun sends us.
17. Take away a tenth of sunlight, and Earth slowly cools by around eight degrees.
18. That's colder than the last ice age, when ice a mile thick sat on top of Canada.
19. And that's before the ice starts helping, because snow is white, and white reflects sunlight back into space.
20. More snow means more cold, which means more snow, and some climate models say it doesn't stop until the whole planet freezes over.
21. Scientists even have a name for that. Snowball Earth.
22. So picture it: a frozen, pink, dim planet, with black forests poking out of the snow.
23. But here's the weird part: the black trees are still growing.
24. You see, a leaf isn't green because it loves green.
25. It feeds on red and blue light, and green is the color it uses least, so it throws it back at you.
26. Take green away, and a leaf has nothing left to throw, so it goes black, and keeps eating red and blue like nothing happened.
27. Every green thing you've ever seen was just light that a plant didn't want.
28. Green was never the trees' color. It was the part they gave to us.
