## Premise
You wake up with green, leaf-like skin that photosynthesizes. Same body, same appetite.

## Facts / own calcs
- About 1 % of daily energy, a tennis court of skin: plant ecologist Lindsay Turnbull (Oxford) in Live Science, "What if humans
  had photosynthetic skin?", for an average adult woman. Other popular estimates agree on the order of magnitude (Naked
  Scientists: ~290 h of strong sun per day of energy; one analysis ~15 kcal per naked hour). A forum post with generous
  assumptions gets ~25 %; not used.
- Own calc: 1 % of ~2,000 kcal = ~20 kcal; a slice of toast ~80 kcal, so about a quarter slice -> "one bite of toast".
- Leaf efficiency: typical crops 1-2 % of sunlight into biomass, peaks 3.5-4.3 % (Wikipedia, Photosynthetic efficiency; Britannica
  calls 1 % exceptional over a year) -> "only about two percent", the generous end.
- Brain: about 20 % of resting energy use -> "about a fifth" (standard physiology figure; NOT re-checked this session, verify a
  source before the description cites it).
- Skin area: adult ~1.7-2 m2 -> "around two square metres" (standard figure, same note).
- Sea slug: Elysia chlorotica keeps stolen chloroplasts working for months without algae (Sci Rep 2017 s41598-017-08002-0
  and reviews). Whether sunlight sustains it is debated: some slugs survived with photosynthesis blocked, and kleptoplasts alone
  cannot support growth in lab conditions (2017 E. viridis study) -> "can go months without a meal", "can't grow on light alone".
  "Solar cells" = the chloroplasts, spoken as a plain image.

## Chain
wake up green -> stand in the sun all day -> 1 % -> because a leaf only manages 2 % -> a tree can afford that -> you can't
(brain, 2 m2) -> you'd need a tennis court -> even the slug can't grow on light -> sunset, toast, one bite = the whole day -> question.
