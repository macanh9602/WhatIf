## Premise
You wake up with working gills on your neck. Lungs still there; nothing else about your body changes.

## Facts / own calcs
- Density: seawater ~1,025 kg/m3, air ~1.2 kg/m3 -> ~850x -> "eight hundred times denser".
- Oxygen: air holds ~30x more O2 than the same volume of water at 15 C (physiology texts; ~275 mg/L vs ~8-10 mg/L).
  Own calc: resting O2 use ~250 mL/min = ~0.33 g/min; seawater ~7.5 mg/L, gills extract ~60 % -> ~75 L/min,
  a 150-200 L bathtub every 2-3 minutes -> "a bathtub of sea ... every few minutes". Water breathers spend ~20 % of
  resting energy on ventilation vs 1-2 % for air breathers.
- Heat: water conducts heat ~24x better than air (0.6 vs 0.025 W/m.K); diving sources say 25x, rescue sources up to 32x
  -> "about twenty-five times faster". Gills are the main heat leak of fish: blood meets water across a thin wall.
- Opah: Wegner et al. 2015, Science 348:786 ("Whole-body endothermy in a mesopelagic fish, the opah"): counter-current heat
  exchangers in the gills, body ~5 C above the water, a warm heart -> "stays just five degrees above the sea".
- Bath: warm water holds less oxygen (~6.5 mg/L at 37 C vs ~9 mg/L at 20 C, fresh) and still water runs out fast,
  hence the aquarium pump. "As hot as you can stand" ~ 40 C.

## Chain
gills -> first breath of sea -> heavy (800x) -> thin (30x less O2, a bathtub every few minutes) -> you manage, the reef
-> you shiver -> because gills are a radiator -> 25x + your warmest blood -> so fish are cold -> even the opah only +5 C
-> back to shore, hot tub + pump -> breathe the bath -> question.
