## Premise
Thanksgiving afternoon, your backyard: you hold a rock-hard frozen turkey over a pot of hot oil while the Neighbor leans on the fence. The turkey does not "explode": the ice on it turns to steam under the oil, the oil is pushed over the rim onto the burner flame, and the fire is the bomb. The safe fact arrives as irony: a fire captain hands you a leaflet that says what the Neighbor already did (thawed bird, nothing in oil), and your turkey is still on the roof. It warns; it never shows or says how to do it (no amounts, no timings, no steps).

## Beats
- Paradox (hook, 12 words): "This turkey is about to become a bomb, and you are holding it." Frame 0: you, mitts on, hoisting a frost-white turkey, the pot behind. The consequence (you about to lower it into hot oil) is in frame within 2 s.
- Plant: "a little coat of frost" on the turkey. It sounds like decoration, even cute. (Silent second plant: a warning sticker on the pot, readable only at the reveal.)
- Mockery: Neighbor: "That bird is cold." You: "It's fine."
- Count up: "The oil bubbles. Then it foams. Then it climbs the pot." The Neighbor steps back, goes inside, locks the door.
- Turn: "But the oil does not stay in the pot." Over the rim, down the side, into the flame. "Whoomp." Fireball taller than the garage; you are behind a lawn chair; "The lawn chair is not."
- You see: "that frost was water." Ice under hot oil becomes steam in a flash, steam needs 1,700 times the room, the oil has nowhere to go but out, to the flame. The frost is re-read: it was the fuse.
- Button: dusk, the fire engine; the captain's count (over fourteen hundred cooking fires on one Thanksgiving); the leaflet "thaw it all the way, and fry it outside, far from the house"; dinner at the Neighbor's: his turkey sat in the fridge until it was ready, "nobody calls it a bomb". "Yours is still on the roof." Reuses "bomb" from the hook. Loop seam: "...still on the roof. | This turkey is about to become a bomb, and you are holding it." Replays cleanly, not one sentence.

## Changed from the user's brief, and why
- **"1,139 cooking fires on Thanksgiving 2024, 321 % of a normal day": not found, replaced.** Every NFPA source I opened gives other numbers. The newest NFPA figure (release dated 2025-11-11, reprinted by the Illinois State Fire Marshal 2025-11-18) is: "An estimated 1,446 home cooking fires were reported to U.S. fire departments on Thanksgiving Day in 2023, reflecting a 388 percent increase over the daily average." The year is 2023, not 2024 (NFPA's 2024 data is not out yet as of 2026-11-08). Earlier NFPA figure: 1,610 in 2022, "399 percent increase over the daily average" (NFPA 2024 release reprinted by Eastie Times, 2024-11-13). So "over 1,400" is true for 2023 and "over 1,000" for every recent year; the script says "over fourteen hundred ... on one Thanksgiving" with no year spoken.
- "% of a normal day": NFPA says "increase over the daily average", so 388 % increase = about 4.9 times the average day, not "321 % of a normal day". Not spoken (the script uses the count only, one number per beat).
- Check again 20-23 Nov: NFPA usually issues its Thanksgiving release in mid-November. If it now gives a 2024 count, update the clipboard (shot 24), the description and this table; keep "over" wording true to the new number.
- "Water expands about 1,700 times as steam": right, with its condition: "At 212 F (100 C) water expands approximately 1,700 times its original volume." The script says "steam needs seventeen hundred times the room" (the room it needs, not that all of it forms at once). Fire Engineering notes that in real fires 10-90 % of the water converts (search snippet, page 403, not opened), so it is a ceiling, and the script does not claim a result of 1,700 times.
- "Bomb" is the hook's hyperbole, and the re-read says the opposite of a blast: the turkey itself does not burst; the oil overflows onto the flame (Hackaday: "hot oil streaming down the sides of the cooking vessel directly towards the gas burner below"). The fireball's size (taller than the garage) is comic scale, not a measured value; description says so.
- Hook lengthened from the user's 8 words to 12 ("..., and you are holding it") because the format gate wants 12-24 and the backlog wants "you" in frame in the first 2 s. The user's sentence is kept whole.
- Safe fact: NFPA's actual advice is stronger than "thaw it": "NFPA strongly discourages the use of turkey fryers that use cooking oil, which can cause devastating burns" (opened). The leaflet line follows the common fire-service advice (thaw completely, fry outdoors away from the house) as the user asked; the button then shows the safest version (the Neighbor's oven). Description carries the NFPA sentence.

## True / invented
"You", the Neighbor, the backyard, the fireball scale, the captain, the leaflet and the pot sticker are invented. Description draft: "The story is made up and the fireball is a cartoon. The facts are real: ice on a turkey turns to steam in hot oil, and at 100 C steam takes up about 1,700 times the volume of the water (Alabama Fire College). NFPA counted an estimated 1,446 home cooking fires on Thanksgiving Day 2023, about 4.9 times an average day, and strongly discourages oil turkey fryers. If you cook a turkey this year, thaw it completely and keep hot oil out of this video's backyard."  (Edit the last sentence to plain advice before publishing; no brand names, no how-to.)
