## Premise
A wrist dial scales your whole body down evenly (same body, same tissue, every length / 170). Not ep02's weight dial: size, not mass.

## Facts / own calcs
- 1.7 m / 170 = 1.0 cm. Area x 1/170^2 = 1/28,900; volume and weight x 1/170^3 = 1/4.91 M -> "about five million times lighter".
- Falls: air drag scales with area, weight with volume (Haldane, "On Being the Right Size", 1926: a mouse dropped down a mine
  shaft walks away). Terminal speed falls roughly with sqrt(size), faster still at insect scale -> "drift down like a leaf".
- Heat: surface per gram x 170 (own calc, geometry only; real metabolic scaling is gentler, which the script does not claim).
- Shrews eat ~1-3x their body weight a day and starve within a few hours without food (National Wildlife Federation, pygmy
  shrew 2010; denali.org). Script: "about your own weight", "miss a few hours of eating, and the cold wins".
- Water: Haldane: a wet mouse carries about its own weight of water; a wetted fly is "in a serious position". At 1 cm you are
  at fly scale -> surface tension holds you in a drop.
- Voice (own estimate): fold length / 170 with the same tissue -> pitch x ~170: 120 Hz -> ~20 kHz. Adults typically hear to
  ~15-17 kHz; cats to ~64 kHz. Spoken as "so high that grown-ups can't hear it", no number.
