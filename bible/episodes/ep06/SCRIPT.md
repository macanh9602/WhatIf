## Premise
An app buys your sleep by the hour at $20. The buyer gets the rest without sleeping; the seller simply goes without it.
The $20 is our invented price, not a fact, and appears on screen only.

## Facts / own calcs
- Lifetime asleep: 8 h / 24 h = 1/3. Over 80 years: 80 / 3 = 26.7 years -> "twenty-six extra years".
- Cost (own calc): 80 y x 365 x 8 h = 233,600 h x $20 = $4.67 M -> "under five million dollars".
- Selling 2 of 8 hours leaves 6 h. Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington & Dinges 2003 (Sleep 26(2):117-126, PMID 12683469):
  14 days at <= 6 h gave deficits equivalent to UP TO 2 nights of total deprivation, and subjects were largely unaware of them.
  A secondary summary says 1-2 days, so the script uses the cautious "a whole night", "within weeks".
- Brain waste: Xie et al. 2013 (Science 342:373): IN MICE, interstitial space grows ~60 % in natural sleep and beta-amyloid
  clears faster. The script says "in mice" and does not speak the 60 %.
- Gardner: 17 years old, 264 h = 11 days 25 min, Dec 1963 - Jan 1964, monitored with William Dement (NPR 2024). Memory and
  judgment suffered. Follow-ups found no significant lasting damage: "started to fail" describes the attempt, not his later life.
  Robert McDonald's 453 h (1986) is a longer Guinness mark, hence "the longest stretch scientists have verified".
- Dolphins: EEG of bottlenose dolphins shows one hemisphere in slow-wave sleep while the other is awake; breathing is
  voluntary (Scientific American). One researcher disputes calling this sleep; the script says "rest half a brain".
- Short sleep: 33.2 % of US adults < 7 h (CDC, BRFSS 2020, Prev Chronic Dis 2023); 35 % in BRFSS 2014; NHIS 2020 is lower at
  ~28 %. "One in three" follows BRFSS and the survey choice matters.

## Chain
sell 2 h, feel fine -> billionaire buys every night (26 years, < $5 M) -> you keep selling because you feel fine
-> tests like a skipped night, unaware -> because sleep is when the brain clears waste -> the poorest sell most, up to the
limit -> teen 1964, 11 days -> dolphins never dropped sleep -> a third of adults already give it away -> question.
