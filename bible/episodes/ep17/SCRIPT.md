## Premise
One morning every person in town carries a floating number over the head: their salary. Nobody's money changes, only what everyone can see, and that alone makes you feel poor. It really happened: Norway put tax records online in 2001 and the happiness gap between richer and poorer grew. Norway's fix was to make looking non-anonymous, and the button gives you the same fix.

## Beats
- Paradox (hook, 13 words): "This morning, a number floats over every head on your street, including yours." The consequence is in frame from frame 0: you on the step, your own tag over your head.
- Plant: the bank app "says the same as yesterday, down to the cent." Looks like a throwaway reassurance.
- Mockery: the Neighbor, under a much bigger number, looks up at yours: "Oh. That's nice."
- Count up: work (the twin with your job and a higher number; you stop saying hi), lunch (the café terrace sorts itself by number), date (she looks above your head first).
- Turn: "But by night, you feel poorer than you have ever felt." The plant line comes back: the bank app still says the same. Same words, new meaning: the money never moved.
- You see: "one country actually tried this." Norway, 2001, income online; nobody's pay changed; the happiness gap between richer and poorer people grew 29 %. The effect was on how people felt, not on money.
- Button: Norway's real fix (searches lose their anonymity, they drop off a cliff) becomes the gag: your tag now says "Seen by: your neighbor"; he waves, you wave; nobody looks over a head anymore. Reuses "head" and "street" from the hook. Loop line: none (the seam "...looks over a head anymore. | This morning, a number floats over every head..." replays cleanly but is not one sentence).

## Changed from the user's draft, and why
- Brief hook "One country actually tried this. People got less happy." Two fixes. (1) "People got less happy" is not what the paper found: it found the GAP in happiness between richer and poorer people widened by 29 % (life satisfaction gap 21 %); it does not claim everyone's happiness fell. (2) Saying the country and the result in the first seconds answers the hook before 20 s and spoils the "You see,". So the hook is the consequence ("a number floats over every head... including yours", 13 words) and "One country actually tried this." opens the reveal at ~56 % of the words.
- Brief "Norway put tax records online in 2001": right (AER abstract: "In 2001, Norwegian tax records became easily accessible online"). Note for the description: the lists show taxable income, wealth and tax paid for the year, not a salary as such; the floating "salary" is our simplification and the script says "income" in the fact line.
- "29 %" is right and is the happiness gap, so the line says "the happiness gap between richer and poorer people grew by twenty nine percent", not "people got 29 % less happy".
- Added Norway's anonymity rule (sourced) as the button's mechanism, because it is the real, ironic end of the story: the cure was being seen looking.

## True / invented
"You", the floating tags, the town, the date, the café and the twin are invented. Description draft: "The floating numbers are made up. The rest is real: in 2001 Norway's tax records went online, and the happiness gap between richer and poorer Norwegians grew 29 % (Perez-Truglia, American Economic Review, 2020). Since 2014 a search is no longer anonymous: the person you look up can see your name, and searches fell by about 90 %."
