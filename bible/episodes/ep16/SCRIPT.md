## Premise
Every object in town grows a tip screen and strikes when you skip it. The joke is the law: a tipped worker's base pay can be $2.13, so the wage sits on the customer, and the screens just ask. You end up on the other side of the screen.

## Beats
- Paradox: "The door just asked you for twenty percent, and you haven't even walked through it yet."
- Plant: the coffee cart's taped sign, "Help wanted. $2.13 an hour." Looks like a typo or a joke.
- Mockery: two layers. You mock the sign ("Who would work for that?"); the Neighbor mocks you ("Just press twenty. Everybody does.").
- Count up: shop door locks, park bench tips you into the grass, bus stop slides its roof away; at home the steps go flat, the elevator stops.
- Turn: "But by bedtime, your card says zero." The bed folds into the wall, on strike.
- You see: the sign was real. Federal law lets a boss pay a tipped worker $2.13 an hour if tips make up the rest (tip credit), so the wage moved onto the customer. (Round 2: the "screens work / 13 million taxi rides" sentences were cut: three spoken numbers within ~10 s.)
- Button: you take the cart job, same pay as 1991, and say the Neighbor's line to your first customer. Reuses "twenty" from the hook. Loop line: none (the seam reads "Everybody does." | "The door just asked you...", fine as a replay but not a sentence join).

## Changed from the user's draft, and why
- Brief fact "Federal minimum for tipped workers can be just $2.13/hour" is right as the CASH wage, but incomplete: the employer must top up to $7.25 when tips fall short (tip credit max $5.12). Script says "as long as tips make up the rest", which keeps it true. Many states set a higher tipped wage; the script says "In America, a boss may", not "everyone earns".
- Brief's "point-of-sale screens default to 18/20/25 %": no source found that these are THE defaults. Screens show 18/20/25 as an on-screen design choice only; no spoken claim. The spoken fact uses the sourced taxi study instead (higher defaults, bigger tips).
- Brief's object order (door, stairs, elevator, bed) kept, but outdoor objects added (bench, bus stop) and the stairs became the apartment's front steps, to reach >= 60 % outdoors.
- The objects are fiction; the screens' logic (customer pays the wage) is the real part. No brands: no payment-terminal logos, plain grey screens.
