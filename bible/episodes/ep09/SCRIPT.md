## Premise
"You" (the extrovert) wake up as a living sea sponge (a demosponge) on a shallow Caribbean seafloor. Same mind for the
narration's sake; the joke is that a real sponge has nothing to feel with.

## Facts / own calcs
- No nerves, no muscles, no organs, no mouth: water enters through pores (ostia), flagellated choanocytes drive it, it leaves
  through the osculum (Britannica, "sponge: functional features").
- Pumping: demosponges filter up to ~900x their body volume per hour (U. Alberta thesis; Suberites concinnus up to ~1000x/h).
  Own calc: a 10 L sponge at ~900x/h = 9 m3/h = ~216 m3/day; a backyard pool ~40-60 m3 -> "a backyard pool's worth ... in a day"
  holds even at a quarter of that rate.
- Sneezing: Kornder et al. 2022, Current Biology, "Sponges sneeze mucus to shed particulate waste from their seawater inlet
  pores": a contraction every ~3-8 h, the mucus is eaten by fish and other animals -> "every few hours", "fish line up to eat it".
- Guests: loggerhead sponge (Spheciospongia vesparium) hosts alpheid snapping shrimp (Synalpheus), plus crabs and worms; numbers
  grow with sponge volume (Westinga & Hoetjes 1981; Gulf of Mexico studies); eusocial S. regalis colonies live in sponge canals.
  The popular "16,000 shrimp" figure is unsourced, so the script says "hundreds".
- Reassembly: H. V. Wilson 1907, Science (volume/pages differ between sources: 25:912 vs 23:91; check before the description cites it) ("A new method by which sponges may be artificially reared"): sponge pressed
  through fine cloth into cells, the cells re-aggregated into new sponges.
