The door just asked you for twenty percent, and you haven't even walked through it yet.
Overnight, every screen in town grew three tip buttons.
The coffee cart has them too, and that's fine: a person made your coffee.
Under the screen hangs a little sign.
"Help wanted. Two dollars and thirteen cents an hour."
You laugh.
"Who would work for that?"
So you skip the tip on the shop door, and it locks.
Then the park bench asks, and tips you into the grass.
Then the bus stop wants a tip for its shade, so the roof slides away.
Your neighbor shakes his head.
"Just press twenty. Everybody does."
At home, the front steps fold flat, and the elevator stops between floors until you pay.
But by bedtime, your card says zero.
So the bed folds into the wall and goes on strike.
You see, that sign was no joke.
In America, a boss may pay a tipped worker just two thirteen an hour, as long as tips make up the rest.
So the wage moved off the boss and onto you.
So next morning, you take the coffee cart job.
Same pay as in 1991.
Your first customer stares at the buttons.
You smile.
"Just press twenty. Everybody does."
