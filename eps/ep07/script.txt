If you could shrink with a dial, you'd survive any fall, then starve by lunch.
Imagine a dial on your wrist, and you turn it down until you're one centimetre tall.
You step off the kitchen counter, drift down like a leaf, and land without a scratch.
You see, shrink a hundred and seventy times, and your skin shrinks by the square, but your weight shrinks by the cube.
You're about five million times lighter, so the air catches you like a speck of dust.
But that same skin now loses heat a hundred and seventy times faster for every gram of you.
To stay warm, you'd have to eat like a shrew, about your own weight in food every day.
Miss a few hours of eating, and the cold wins.
Then the tap drips, and one drop of water wraps around you like a wet blanket you can't lift.
You shout for help, but your tiny vocal cords push your voice so high that grown-ups can't hear it.
Only the cat can, and the cat is now very interested.
So you spin the dial back up, and the first thing you do at full size is make a giant lunch.
So how small would you dare to go?
